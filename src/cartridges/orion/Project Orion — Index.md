# Project Orion — Index

*A field guide to the Shattered Worlds, and the road through Belldor*

---

## The Short Version

The worlds were once linked by waygates. Then something was taken that should have stayed, and the links began to drift. You are a pilot: a being no world claims, immune to the æther between worlds, and able to bond with the war machines called mechs. Every faction wants you. None of them trust you.

This guide walks the road as it is currently planned, from your first step in the bog to the edge of what has been built. Past that point, the guide says **more to come**.

---

## Part I — The World

### The Waygates

At the heart of every known world stands a waygate, a colossal archway of stone, root and mineral that looks more like a canyon mouth than a building. There are no controls and no inscriptions. You walk through, and the gate reads your ætheric signature and carries you on. No one knows how it picks a destination. No one knows who built them. Scholars believe they were anchors that kept the worlds in alignment, and that belief fits what happened next.

### The Æther

Between the worlds lies the æther, an active and hostile medium. Living tissue that touches it disintegrates at once. The proof is infamous: on an interworld vessel, a single strand of an elven bride's hair crossed a flux-field breach, and she died instantly. Her death was taken for murder, and two elven clans fought an eight-year war before the truth came out.

Vessels can cross the æther only by holding a protective flux field around the hull. The æther can be resisted. It has never been tamed.

### The Mining Operation

A dwarven civilization once mined a rare crystal. Nothing happened at first. Then landscapes shifted without cause, ruins appeared where none had stood, and the stars stopped matching the records. The crystals were **tuning crystals**, calibration structures that kept the worlds synchronized.

> Worlds did not break. They drifted.

This drift is called **desynchronization**, and it is still happening.

### The Pilots

Pilots appeared only after the catastrophe. No world claims them, and no record of them exists before it. They can walk through the æther unprotected and bond with mechs at once. People say they are remnants of the catastrophe, or echoes, or walking fractures in reality. Nobody has proven anything. Factions recruit them anyway.

### The Fragmented Present

Distances between worlds now fluctuate. Some worlds drift closer, and others recede out of reach. The powers that thrive are the ones holding waygates, interworld vessels and strategic resources. The ones that struggle are cut off from their trade routes or stranded on drifting worlds. Some worlds have simply gone silent. War is constant.

## Part III — Belldor

> **Belldor teaches you how to fight. Every other world tests whether you learned.**

Before the catastrophe, Camelot was a center of magic and governance with one of the largest waygates in the network. Now regions of it phase in and out of alignment, cities flicker between ruin and restoration, and its ruling authority is gone. Several factions fight over the waygate, and all of them recruit pilots.

The campaign on Belldor is built as a lesson. You are never handed a tool before the world gives you a reason to need it, and the numbers on your mech do not grow. Two pilots at the same stage are equally strong, and what separates them is what they choose to do.

### Choosing Your Starting Loadout Kit (`v0.8` Classless Spec)

Your mech is a modular war machine, and **Project Orion has no player-facing classes**. Six pre-configured **Starting Loadout Kits** are available at Pilot Creation so you begin with a coherent frame, reactor, and weapon pairing—none of them locks you in. Any pilot can equip any weapon, coreplate, utility system, or feat as long as they meet the hardware and gemstone requirements. See `Project Orion — Equipment & Feats.md` for the full catalog of 29 magitech weapons, 6 coreplates, 6 reactors, 18 aux/utility systems, 18 optics/sensors, 12 limb actuators, and 97 feats.

#### The Three Layers of a Build (`Pilot → Frame → Equipment`)
- **Pilot (`Skills`)**: Created at New Game (`Starting Kit → Pilot Callsign → Level 1 in M00 The Bog`) and listed on the Load Game roster. Six **Combat Disciplines** progress through gemstone investment (locked out during the `Levels 1–5` tutorial until `Level 6: Border Town Complete`) and unlock **Feat Tiers (`I–V`)**.
- **Frame (`Feats`)**: **97 Canonical Feats** across Tiers I–V. **System Feats** step up Load Capacity (`light → medium → heavy → superheavy`); **Interface Feats** tune combat behavior and weapon/hardware synergies within a fixed static budget. Feats are gated by Skill Tier, Gemstone sockets, and equipped Hardware/Weapon families—never by class—and can be unsocketed and refunded at any time.
- **Equipment (`13 Slots + Mods`)**: Weapon Set 1 (`Weapon 1–2`), Weapon Set 2 (`Weapon 3–4`), Tactical Utility (`1`, `Action 6`), Aux (`Aux 1–4`, `Actions 7–10`), Belt (`4`, `Actions 7–10`), plus `Coreplate`, `Reactor Core`, `Optics`, `Sensory A/B`, `Arms`, and `Legs`. Action Modifications alter behavior with a paired drawback.

### The Æther Fog

Belldor does not hold still. Beyond the places that were built, the land frays into **æther fog**: stretches of the same ruined country that shift, fold back on themselves and refuse to stay mapped. It is what desynchronization feels like from the ground. Nothing in it is fixed, and a pilot who has been through it once cannot be sure the same road will be there twice. The fog is also what makes the world larger than it looks. There is no wall at the edge of the map, only more fog.

**Density.** The fog gets thicker the farther in you go, and everything gets worse with it. Enemies grow more aggressive, the mist hangs heavier, and the ground becomes harder to cross. Pilots are immune to the æther itself, so the æther is not what hurts you. The danger is the environment it creates, and nothing in it is safe.

**The path.** The fog is built around authored places: towns, boss sanctums, waystations and waygates. Between them lie corridors that carry you from one place to the next, with branches off to side pockets that hold a reward, a fight or a hidden switch. The game nudges you along the path, but you never have to follow it. You can wander the fog all day if you want to.

**Going deeper.** Some branches lead into the fog proper, and each room is one step deeper than the last. Until you reach the deepest room, you can always turn around and retrace your steps, and every room back toward the path is as safe as it was when you passed through. The deepest room is different. It holds a boss that must be cleared, and clearing it returns you to the last place you stood on the main path. The depth where the boss waits is not the same everywhere. A shallow pocket may bottom out after two or three rooms, and a deep one after five or more.

**The Lantern.** The Poe's gift is the one tool that reads the fog. It shows where the fog deepens and what is waiting beneath the water, and it never points you toward the story.

## Part IV — The Road Through Belldor

### Stage 1: The Bog (Levels 1–2)

You arrive in a drowned maze of fallen trees and the ruins of an old temple or monastery. The air is thick with æther. The water is not the æther's doing, but it is dangerous in its own right: **bog water is corrosive**. It eats armor, and it does not care who is standing in it. That includes the things hunting you. The marsh lies inside Belldor's æther fog, so the two overlap here, but they are separate things. The bog is not æther, and not every stretch of fog holds bog water.

**What lives here**

- **Orc pairs.** Orcs always come as a Warrior and a Scout. The Warrior is a shield-bearing brawler who closes distance and hits hard. The Scout stays back, shoots from range, and tries to slip away to heal if you hurt it. Kill the Scout first, but don't let the Warrior reach you.
- **The Bog Wurm.** A snake-like hybrid of machine and flesh, with glowing ætheric veins running through its mechanical parts. It hides invisible beneath the water, strikes first, and retreats to heal when hurt. The bog is its friend, and it knows it.

**The Poe.** Somewhere in the maze you will find a small floating lantern-spirit that calls itself "Poe." Every Poe says the same thing about itself, and they only manifest where the æther is thick. Helping it back to its lantern will earn you a gift. It reveals what hides beneath the water and where the fog grows deeper, and it will change how you fight the Wurm. Ignoring it or killing it is also allowed. The way out of the bog stays hidden until you deal with it one way or another.

**Bedivere.** Further on lies the body of a knight who was heading for the border town and never made it. He carries a **signet ring** and a **numbered list** of items that makes no sense yet. Taking the ring has consequences in the next zone. Leaving it has consequences too. The game will not explain the weight of that choice.

### Stage 2: Camelot Border Town (Levels 3–6)

The road out of the bog drops you at the outer edge of a large contested settlement, the gateway between orc territory and Camelot's interior. No one governs it, and no part of it is safe.

**The sickness.** Everyone here suffers from **grail sickness**. Some people are calm enough to trade. Some will turn on you if you linger. Some are feral and attack on sight. Which is which is not always obvious.

**The ring.** Knights' loyalists in town see anyone carrying Bedivere's ring as a grave robber. If you took it, loyalist merchants will not deal with you, and some will attack outright. Non-loyalists don't care either way. The ring is also your key to the dungeon subplot.

**The districts**

| District | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Outer Perimeter** | Collapsed walls, abandoned orc siege gear, burnt waystations. Where you arrive and the first line against invasion. |
| **Market District** | A half-working market where buildings flicker between intact and ruined every few seconds and the day-night cycle races. Container caches hold parts, consumables and blueprints. |
| **Barracks Quarter** | The former knight garrison, now a factional holdout. Strong fortifications, better loot, and the patrol routes of the three knights. |
| **Cathedral District** | Burnt grail tapestries, a defiled altar, bells that ring out of rhythm. The place to learn what the catastrophe did to Camelot. |

**Orc raids.** The orcs of the nearby encampment raid the town without warning. A war horn sounds, the screen pulses red, and the exit back to the bog is sealed until the raid is dealt with. During a raid **every NPC goes feral**. The orcs go after NPCs before they go after you, so you can fight for the town, stay out of it, or run for the dungeon and leave the market to be looted. If you save enough people, the survivors will remember it.

**The Three Knights**

| Knight | Fights as | What to expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Percival**, Grail Knight | A guardian. A shield wall that has to be flanked or broken, and a charge that closes distance. | Protects his comrades. Recovers shields when badly hurt, so don't let up. |
| **Tristan**, Corrupted Ranger | A kiting archer. Volleys of arrows, smoke to escape corners, and a sight that finds you behind cover. | Fragile if you catch him. Punishes anyone who sits still. |
| **Bors**, Berserk Striker | A relentless brawler who grows stronger with every hit he lands. | Turns enraged at half health. His killing lunge is telegraphed, and deadly if you miss it. |

You can take them one at a time, or all at once for a better reward, a feat called **Knight's Oath**, which gives permanent bonus damage against Camelot's enemies.

**The Dungeon Door.** At the edge of the cathedral a stone staircase descends into mist, past carved grail symbols and flickering statues. This is the last gate before the Camelot waygate, and there are three planned ways to open it:

- **By force.** Defeat all three knights and take a Grail Fragment from each.
- **By exploration.** Find three hidden switches: one behind a desynchronizing wall in the market, one under the cathedral altar, and one in a secret armory room in the barracks.
- **By trust.** Earn the goodwill of the town through enough successful raid defenses. Carrying the ring makes this far harder.

Which of these will be live in the final game is still being decided.

### Stage 3: What Waits Below

Below the staircase lie lore, the key to the Camelot waygate, and the end of your first road. **Planned, but not yet designed.**

## Part V — Secrets

Some of what Belldor holds is not on the main road.

- **Bedivere's list.** Its items read as nonsense. They are scattered across more than one zone. Whoever collects them all and returns to the bog will find the water has been waiting. Something half machine and half serpent, an old name twisted by the æther, answers to the **Lady of the Lake**.
- **Hidden caches** in the border town hold blueprints, and every piece of gear in this game has to be found, crafted or earned.

---

## Part VI — After Belldor

By the time you are done here, you will have everything the game has to teach. Your frame, your weapons and your skills are all yours. From then on, progress is about **how** you fight and not how strong you are. A new weapon moves differently and does not hit harder, and different worlds ask different things of the same pilot.

**More to come:**

- **The Orc Encampment** where the raids begin.
- **The deep dungeon** beneath the border town, and the **Camelot waygate**.
- **Camelot's interior** beyond the gate.
- **The other worlds**: Tyrranus, Orion, Novarin, Chyme, Golem, New Novarin and Sin, plus the minor and unstable worlds.
- **Æther incursions.** Pilots can project their mechs across the æther into other pilots' worlds, as defenders or as attackers. The first incursion is an opt-in event somewhere after Belldor, and you can switch back out at any time with no penalty.
- **Queued 3v3 and 5v5 modes**, unlocked by exploration rather than level.
- **The truth about the pilots**, the waygates and the tuning crystals.

> The truth is not lost. It is unfolding.
